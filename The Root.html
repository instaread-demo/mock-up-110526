    Here are The Biggest Flipped Seats of Midterm 2026

    Somebody check on Capitol Hill. Democrats took both chambers of Congress, but the 2026 midterms delivered a political earthquake well beyond the Beltway. Black candidates didn’t just show up—they made history. With barrier-breaking wins from Keisha Lance Bottoms, Aaron Ford, and Penny Brown Reynolds, long-standing political firsts became election-night realities. From flipping longtime red strongholds to redrawing the national map, here are the biggest flipped seats as called by the Associated Press. Just keep reading.

    Keisha Lance Bottoms

    Georgia just elected Keisha Lance Bottoms as its first Black governor and the first Black woman governor in American history. Her victory ended nearly three decades of Republican control in the statehouse. More than a simple party flip, it marked a historic, barrier-breaking milestone in U.S. politics.

    Gina Hinojosa

    Gina Hinojosa defeated three-term Republican Greg Abbott, becoming Texas’ first Latina governor and the first Democrat elected governor since Ann Richards in 1990. In a state Republicans had dominated for decades, this was a generational political shift and a historic breakthrough for Latina representation.

    Aaron Ford

    Aaron Ford defeated Republican incumbent Joe Lombardo, becoming Nevada’s first Black governor. His victory added another Black governor to the nation’s political landscape and flipped a Republican-held governorship. The significance extends beyond Nevada: Black political leadership is no longer an exception in gubernatorial politics.

    Josh Turek

    Josh Turek defeated Republican Ashley Hinson, flipping Iowa’s open Senate seat. A former Paralympic basketball player, Turek becomes the first former Paralympian elected to the U.S. Senate. Iowa also elected a Democratic senator for the first time since 2008.

    David Crowley

    Milwaukee County Executive David Crowley made history as Wisconsin’s first Black governor. His win flipped the Republican-held office and placed a Black executive at the helm of a critical presidential battleground state. Representation and political geography collided in one consequential victory.

    Amy Acton

    Amy Acton defeated Republican Vivek Ramaswamy, becoming Ohio’s first Democratic governor since 2006. Her victory ends two decades of Republican gubernatorial control and gives Democrats another major Midwestern governorship. It’s a significant reversal in a state that has moved toward Republicans in recent presidential elections.

    Troy Jackson

    Troy Jackson defeated longtime Republican Sen. Susan Collins, ending her decades-long Senate career and flipping a seat Democrats had not won since 1988. The result is particularly significant because Collins had repeatedly survived Democratic challenges in a state that votes Democratic in presidential elections.

    Adam Hamilton

    Adam Hamilton defeated incumbent Republican Roger Marshall, making history in a state that had not elected a Democratic U.S. senator since 1932. That’s nearly a century of Republican dominance in Kansas’ Senate representation interrupted by one election.

    James Talarico

    James Talarico defeated Republican Ken Paxton, giving Texas Democrats their first Senate victory since 1988. He also became part of a broader Democratic breakthrough in statewide Texas politics, alongside Gina Hinojosa’s gubernatorial victory. Two statewide victories in one election fundamentally changed the state’s political narrative.

    Roy Cooper

    Former governor Roy Cooper defeated Republican Michael Whatley to claim North Carolina’s open Senate seat. Democrats hadn’t won a Senate race in the state since 2008, or an open Senate seat since 1974. That’s a 52-year drought for open-seat victories finally ending.

    Annie Andrews

    Dr. Annie Andrews defeated Darline Graham, flipping a South Carolina Senate seat held by Republicans since Strom Thurmond switched parties in 1964. She also becomes the first woman elected to represent South Carolina in the U.S. Senate. A remarkable breakthrough in one of the South’s most reliably Republican states.

    Sherrod Brown

    Sherrod Brown defeated Republican Jon Husted, reclaiming an Ohio Senate seat after losing his previous reelection bid in 2024. The former senator’s return flips a Republican-held seat and restores a familiar Democratic figure to Washington. A comeback with real consequences for Senate control.

    Penny Brown Reynolds

    Penny Brown Reynolds defeated Republican Tim Fleming, becoming Georgia’s first Black secretary of state and the first Democrat to hold the office in nearly 20 years. A former judge and Biden administration official, Reynolds’ victory marks a historic breakthrough for Black women in statewide elected leadership.
