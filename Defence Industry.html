Royal Air Force says Protector reaches 1,000 operational flying hours and begins carrying guided weapons

By Martin Chomsky (Defence Industry Europe)

Unmanned systems | November 4, 2026

Photo: Royal Air Force (RAF).

The Royal Air Force’s Protector remotely piloted aircraft has completed 1,000 operational flying hours since its first operation in November 2025. The RAF said the aircraft has also begun carrying MBDA Brimstone 3A missiles and Raytheon UK Paveway IV precision-guided weapons.

Protector provided intelligence during the recent escalation in the Middle East, supporting efforts to protect RAF personnel, UK allies and partners. Its missions delivered overwatch and timely information to senior decision-makers, according to the service.

“Protector has already proven to be a vital Intelligence, Surveillance, Target Acquisition and Reconnaissance asset since its first operational flight in 2025,” said Air Marshal Mark Flewin, Air and Space Commander. “It played a key role in supporting the protection of RAF personnel, UK allies, and partners, during the recent escalation in the Middle East by delivering critical overwatch and timely information to senior decision makers.”

The medium-altitude, long-endurance aircraft, designated Protector RG1 Mk1 (MQ-9B), forms part of the RAF’s intelligence, surveillance, target acquisition and reconnaissance fleet. The addition of precision-guided weapons broadens its role beyond gathering intelligence.

“Protector is a powerful example of the future of the RAF, harnessing cutting-edge technology to give our Armed Forces a vital operational advantage,” said Armed Forces Minister Louise Sandher-Jones. “It has already provided vital intelligence during recent tensions in the Middle East, and its success is testament to the highly skilled personnel who operate and maintain it.”
