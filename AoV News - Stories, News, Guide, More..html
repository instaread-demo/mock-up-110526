The Tokyogurl Scandal: Fake Careers & Fallout in Women’s Esports

November 2, 2026 admin

The 2025 Southeast Asian Games (SEA Games 33) in Bangkok saw one of the most unprecedented cheating scandals in competitive gaming history. What began as a high-profile disqualification quickly turned into a criminal conviction and a setback for the perception of women’s esports.

1. How the Scheme Unraveled

  • The Incident (Dec 15, 2025): Representing host nation Thailand in the women’s Arena of Valor (RoV) semi-final against Vietnam, Warasin “Tokyogurl” Naraphat raised suspicions when officials noticed severe delays and discrepancies between her physical hand movements on her tablet and her character’s movement on screen.
  • The Technique: Referees discovered that two separate devices were logged into Tokyogurl’s tournament account. She was receiving a remote screen-share via Discord while pretending to play, allowing her boyfriend—former AoV professional player Kong “Cheerio” Sutprom—to play the match on her behalf.
  • On-Air Misconduct: Amid suspicions and a mounting loss, Tokyogurl drew further outrage by making an unsportsmanlike gesture (flipping off) directly at the broadcast camera.

2. Institutional Response & Legal Consequences

Following an investigation by Thailand’s Central Investigation Bureau (CIB) and publisher Garena, severe penalties were issued:

│                    PENALTIES & CONSEQUENCES                 │
├──────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────────┤
│ Publisher Sanctions      │ Lifetime competitive ban (Garena)│
│ Organizational Action    │ Contract terminated (Talon TH)   │
│ National Team Result     │ Team Thailand withdrawn          │

  • Team Withdrawal: The Thailand E-Sports Federation (TESF) pulled the entire women’s AoV squad from the SEA Games in disgrace, forfeiting their shot at a medal.
  • Publisher & Team Ban: Publisher Garena issued a lifetime ban from all sanctioned tournaments, and Talon Esports terminated her sponsorship contract immediately.
  • Criminal Prosecution: In March 2026, the Pathumwan Kwaeng Court convicted both Naraphat and Sutprom for unlawful access to protected computer data. During the trial, Sutprom confessed that he had been secretly playing on her account for years to boost her streaming persona and social media presence. Both received three-month detention sentences and fines.

3. Impact on Women’s Esports

The aftermath extended far beyond one player’s fraudulent career, casting a shadow over the broader women’s competitive scene:

  • Fueling Sexist Tropes: Bad-faith actors across online gaming communities used the incident to validate misogynistic stereotypes regarding “fake female gamers” playing solely for male attention or clout.
  • Damage to Legitimacy: The scandal overshadowed legitimate, highly skilled female competitors who already face systemic hurdles—such as lower tournament prize pools, lack of organizational backing, and tokenistic advocacy from esports brands.
  • National Embarrassment: As the host nation of SEA Games 33, Thailand’s esports federation faced severe reputational damage, prompting public apologies from national team officials.
